# MORPH-GEN Theory

## 1. Behavioral generator basis

Let a deterministic symbolic system be

\[
S=(X,U,T,O,I,R),
\]

where `I` and `R` are the initial and reachable predicates. A Boolean vector

\[
F=(f_1,\ldots,f_k):X\to\{0,1\}^k
\]

is a **behavioral generator basis** when there are circuits

\[
G:\{0,1\}^k\times U\to\{0,1\}^k,
\qquad H:\{0,1\}^k\to O
\]

such that, for every reachable microstate and every input,

\[
F(T(x,u))=G(F(x),u),\qquad O(x)=H(F(x)).
\]

It is behaviorally complete when

\[
F(x_1)=F(x_2)
\iff
\forall w\in U^*.\ O(T^*(x_1,w))=O(T^*(x_2,w)).
\]

The fixed gate basis in this repository is `AND`, `XOR`, `NOT`, and `MUX`.
For a hash-consed shared circuit representation,

\[
\operatorname{BGC}(S)=
\min_{F,G,H}
\bigl(|F|_{AIG}+|G|_{AIG}+|H|_{AIG}\bigr).
\]

Proof objects and proof-checking circuits are reported separately. BDD node
counts are backend/order resource measurements, not BGC and not an intrinsic
system invariant.

## 2. Generator re-atomization theorem

**Theorem 1.** If F, G, and H satisfy initial preservation, output preservation,
and transition commutation on R, replacing the micro system by the macro machine
Q=(F,G,H) preserves every future output trace.

**Proof.** Let x_0 satisfy I and set z_0=F(x_0). Initial preservation makes z_0
a legal macro initial state. Inductively assume z_t=F(x_t). Output preservation
gives H(z_t)=H(F(x_t))=O(x_t). For input u_t,

\[
z_{t+1}=G(z_t,u_t)=G(F(x_t),u_t)=F(T(x_t,u_t))=F(x_{t+1}).
\]

Thus the states remain related and outputs agree at every finite time. Equality
of all infinite traces follows by equality of all finite prefixes. ∎

This is the standard homomorphism/coalgebra-morphism correctness argument; the
project does not claim it as a new theorem.

## 3. Generator algebra and the minimal quotient

For each finite input word w and output coordinate j, define the future-output
predicate

\[
p_{w,j}(x)=O_j(T^*(x,w)).
\]

Let A be the Boolean algebra generated by all p_{w,j}. Two reachable states lie
in the same atom of A exactly when every future-output predicate agrees, which
is the deterministic Moore/Myhill–Nerode behavioral equivalence. Consequently:

**Theorem 2.** Atoms of the least transition-closed Boolean algebra containing
the output predicates are in bijection with states of the canonical reachable
minimal behavior quotient.

This is a finite-state restatement of established Myhill–Nerode and coalgebraic
minimization facts. In dual language, predicates form an observable/definable
algebra; for linear systems its degree-one fragment is the dual observable
subspace (an M-invariant subspace in Boolean-network terminology). MORPH-GEN's
engineering contribution is to synthesize a compact shared circuit basis for
that algebra under unknown coordinates and attach independently checkable
certificates, not to rename the underlying correspondence.

## 4. Structural non-identifiability

`results_gen/counterexamples/structural_nonidentifiability.json` contains two
two-bit Moore systems with:

- exactly the same two cross edges;
- the same node and edge counts;
- identical in/out degree sequence;
- one SCC of size two;
- the same output function and initial state.

System A updates `(x0,x1) <- (x1,x0)` and has a one-state reachable quotient
from 00. System B updates `(x0,x1) <- (not x1,x0)` and has a four-state quotient
from the same initial state. The JSON includes complete transition/output tables
and an exhaustive canonical minimization check.

**Theorem 3.** No method that reads only the dependency graph, SCCs, degrees,
or graph communities can be complete for exact macrostate discovery.

**Proof.** Such a method receives identical structural input for A and B and
must return the same answer. Their canonical behavioral quotients have different
cardinality, so one answer cannot be exact for both. ∎

Structure remains useful for proposing regions; it cannot replace semantic
proof.

## 5. Coordinate-conjugacy invariance

Let phi be a bijection on the microstate set and define

\[
T_\phi=\phi\circ T\circ\phi^{-1},
\qquad O_\phi=O\circ\phi^{-1}.
\]

For every input word w, induction gives

\[
T_\phi^*(\phi(x),w)=\phi(T^*(x,w)).
\]

Therefore x and y have identical future traces in S iff phi(x) and phi(y) do
in S_phi. The map `[x] -> [phi(x)]` is a well-defined bijection between
behavioral quotient classes and commutes with transitions and outputs.

**Theorem 4.** Canonical minimal macro machines of conjugate systems are
isomorphic.

The theorem guarantees semantic invariance, not resource invariance. Resource
usage depends on the circuit complexity of phi and on the chosen synthesis
backend. The experiments separately measure affine, triangular, and Feistel
encodings rather than assuming equal cost.

## 6. Polynomial recovery for affine systems

Consider the total Boolean affine system

\[
x'=Ax+Bu+a,\qquad y=Cx+c
\]

over GF(2). Let V be the least row space containing rows of C and closed under
right multiplication by A:

\[
V=\operatorname{span}\{CA^t:t\ge0\}.
\]

The iterative algorithm starts with rows of C and adds vA whenever independent.
It terminates after at most n rank increases. Let rows of F be a basis of V.
Closure yields matrices D,E and d with

\[
FA=DF,qquad FB=E,qquad Fa=d,
\]

so G(z,u)=Dz+Eu+d. Since rows of C lie in V, C=HF for an output decoder H.

The kernel of F is the classical unobservable subspace. Two states have the
same F value iff their difference is unobservable under every future input;
therefore F is behaviorally complete and its rank is minimal among affine
generators. Bitset Gaussian elimination and row multiplication are polynomial
(the implementation uses Python integer bitsets; a conservative bound is
O(n^4) bit operations and O(n^2) stored bits for a dense explicit A).

**Theorem 5A.** A minimal affine behavioral generator basis is recoverable in
polynomial time without enumerating 2^n microstates.

This is based on classical observability, not claimed as a new linear-systems
theorem. The difference from an explicit Boolean-control-network STP algorithm
is representation: MORPH-GEN operates on shared Boolean circuits/bitsets and
never constructs the 2^n-dimensional logical state vector or its 2^n by 2^n
transition matrix.

## 7. Polynomial recovery under a triangular sparse-ANF condition

The implemented bounded-degree result uses a precise stronger condition than
"degree at most d" alone.

Assume the exact future-predicate closure P has k selected ANFs q_i such that:

1. every q_i has degree <= d and at most s monomials;
2. q_i contains a linear pivot variable v_i;
3. v_i occurs in no other selected q_j;
4. after substituting v_i = z_i XOR (q_i XOR v_i), every predicate in P depends
   only on z_1,...,z_k;
5. all 2^k z valuations are feasible (or the feasible set is explicitly
   projected and certified).

The pivots make the substitution triangular and invertible. Sparse ANF
normalization checks closure and removes nuisance variables exactly. Evaluating
the transformed predicates over 2^k macro valuations constructs G and H.

**Theorem 5B.** Under conditions 1–5, the algorithm recovers an exact k-bit
generator in time polynomial in the input circuit size, number of closure
predicates, n^d, s, and 2^k, without enumerating 2^n microstates.

**Proof sketch.** Degree-d monomials range over O(n^d) candidates. Hash-consed
ANF addition/multiplication and pivot substitution are polynomial in the stated
sparse representation bound. Conditions 2–4 prove every closure predicate is a
function of z; condition 5 supplies the exact macro domain. Table construction
enumerates only 2^k macro valuations. Output and transition commutation follow
from closure; behavioral completeness follows because P is the full future-
output closure. ∎

Without the pivot/sparsity condition, bounded degree alone is not sufficient to
guarantee this algorithmic bound: substitution can cause monomial explosion and
coordinate-free recovery can require a different representation. That broader
claim remains INCONCLUSIVE rather than being inferred from the positive SLO
family.

## 8. Certificate logic

Every installed basis carries:

1. initial preservation;
2. output preservation;
3. transition commutation;
4. functional macro transition;
5. reachable coverage and macro surjectivity;
6. pairwise macro distinguishing words;
7. explicit canonical-quotient isomorphism when micro bits <=20;
8. BDD identity results;
9. independently assembled Z3/Tseitin or normalized GF(2) residual checks.

Output/transition homomorphism proves equal F values are behaviorally equivalent.
Pairwise macro distinguishability proves different reachable F values are not
equivalent. Together they establish behavioral completeness.

## 9. Scope

The theory supports exact affine recovery and the stated pivot-triangular ANF
class. Generic shared-AIG CEGIS is exact when it terminates and verifies, but no
polynomial worst-case guarantee is claimed. Feistel scaling exposes the expected
representation-sensitive boundary and is retained as a negative result. BGC is
an explicit-circuit objective; current synthesis proves minimal macro bit count
and macro behavioral minimality, but does not claim a globally minimum AIG gate
count in every non-affine instance.
